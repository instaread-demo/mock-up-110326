Hello, gorgeous! 7 ways to give your home’s entrance some serious wow

Listen to audio version of this article
Sponsored by
0:00 / 7:25
Instaread

Is your entrance giving grand designs or grand disappointment? We’ve grilled the experts at Urban Front for some clever tricks to seriously up your home’s kerb appeal.

By Marie Dorking | 21 October 2026
Share this story:

Got Pinterest boards dedicated to every room in the house, but haven’t given your entrance space a second thought? Same. The good news? You don’t need a Grand Designs budget (or Kevin McCloud on speed dial) to give it some serious wow factor. We’ve grilled the design experts at The House That Muddy Built partners Urban Front for their smartest tricks to give your entrance a little pizzazz – we’re talking bold design choices, clever use of light and the little details that make a surprisingly big difference.

Give your door main character energy

Who says your front door has to play it safe? Whether it’s a punchy splash of colour, a striking contemporary design or honey-hued timber, the entrance to your home is a prime opportunity to show off a little personality. But before you order that hot-pink or acid yellow door, take a good look at your home’s architecture and existing materials. A little contrast can be fabulous; a door that looks like it belongs to an entirely different house? Less so.

Elizabeth Assaf, co-founder and designer at Urban Front, recommends putting together a moodboard of the materials already used on or planned for your home’s exterior before choosing your door. And don’t be afraid to introduce something new, provided the tones work together. Elizabeth is particularly fond of mixing wood and bronze together. “Design should be personal and doors are no different,” she adds.

Her ultimate test? “You want visitors to look at your front door and think, ‘Wow, that’s just so special’.” Ideally, she says, they’ll even want to stroke it. Yes, really!

Show your true colours

Step away from the greige. A zingy yellow, jewel-inspired teal or a bold (and very on-trend) burgundy can transform an otherwise unremarkable entrance into something really rather fabulous. Not feeling quite so brave? A sophisticated charcoal or soft neutral can look just as striking. Not every front door needs to be the life and soul of the street.

And here’s a clever colour tip from the experts at Urban Front: your door doesn’t have to match your windows or garage. In fact, choosing a contrasting shade (or even going a few tones lighter or darker) can make much more of a statement. They suggest thinking of your house as the sofa and your front door as the fabulous scatter cushion that gives the whole thing a lift.

Go big, or go home?

Size isn’t everything. Except, sometimes, it kind of is. An oversized front door can bring instant architectural drama, with Elizabeth suggesting you go big, where possible (“Some architects design doors too small,” she caveats). But equally you don’t need a palatial entrance to make an impression – sometimes it’s about working smarter with the space you’ve got.

“Small spaces can be mighty, and small doors can be mighty too if chosen correctly,” says Elizabeth. Her top tip? Don’t automatically assume you need double doors or glazing down the sides. Sometimes using the whole opening for one larger door, with glazing built in, can make a much bigger impact. And before you go supersized, check how the door will sit alongside your windows and the rest of the façade. Bigger isn’t necessarily better if the proportions are all wrong.

Plant some kerb appeal

Entrance looking a little underwhelming? Before you start planning a full-scale makeover, try planting a little strategic greenery. A pair of oversized planters, some sculptural foliage or a climber scrambling up the brickwork can take your entrance from meh to mega chic.

Urban Front recommends going big with architectural plants and choosing evergreens so your entrance looks the business all year round (even when the rest of the garden’s looking a bit sorry for itself).

And don’t forget the backdrop. Lush foliage looks particularly fabulous against natural materials such as stone, brick and a timber door. Urban Front are big fans of the real wood deal, thanks to its warmth, rich texture and natural grain, which gives every door its own unique character.

Let there be light

Want your entrance to look just as fabulous after dark? Get clever with your outdoor lighting. Urban Front say lighting can make an enormous difference to a home’s frontage, particularly when it’s used to draw attention to its best bits. Strategically placed uplighters can show off beautiful brickwork, architectural features or statement planting (see previous tip!), while stylish wall lights make the approach to your front door feel waaay more inviting, and will also save you fumbling around for your keys in the dark.

And don’t forget what happens once you step inside. If your hallway feels more gloomy cupboard than grand entrance it’s time to get your glow on. Elizabeth recommends thinking carefully about how light enters the hallway when choosing your door. Glazing within or alongside your front door can flood the space with natural light, while a statement pendant or layered wall lighting will keep things cosy when the sun goes down.

Worried about giving the neighbours a front-row seat to your hallway shenanigans? Frosted or sandblasted glazing lets in the daylight without putting your home on display.

It’s all in the details

It’s the little things that can take an entrance space from perfectly nice to properly impressive. We’re talking gorgeous knockers (oo er!), stylish house numbers, a statement letterbox and door furniture that’s every bit as considered as the door itself. The trick? Choosing finishes that work together rather than chucking every passing trend into the mix.

“Design is everything – you want a design that doesn’t go out of fashion in five to ten years,” explains Elizabeth, who recommends timeless choices with a little personality. Think bronze or black handles, an unexpected handle position or even a shorter design to stop your entrance looking more corporate HQ than home.

And don’t be afraid to go bold. Elizabeth says a bright yellow door can look beautifully elegant with the right hardware and styling. Bring on the ochre!

Don’t forget the inside job

OK, so you’ve nailed the kerb appeal. The neighbours are impressed, but what about the view from the other side of the front door? No point wowing the street if your hallway lets the side down.

“Always treat the hallway like an actual room and not just a space connecting rooms,” Elizabeth advises. Think about how you actually use the space: a mirror for that last-minute lipstick check, a console table for the post, somewhere to sit while wrestling on your Uggs and, ideally, somewhere to hide the inevitable shoe and bag clutter.

And while it’s worth making sure your front door works with the flooring, staircase and other finishes in your hallway, Elizabeth says there’s no need to go full matchy, matchy with the outside. In fact, Urban Front offers doors with different finishes on either side, so you can choose one look for the exterior and another for the hallway. Her tip? You’ll spend far more time looking at your front door from inside your home than from the street, so if there’s a finish you particularly love, save it for the inside. Clever, eh?
