‘Will never be forgotten’: Retired Libertyville deputy police chief who served 41 years in law enforcement dies at 82

by Sam Borcia Published November 2, 2026 8:51 PM
Listen to audio version of this article
Sponsored by
0:00 / 3:12

The Libertyville Police Department is mourning a retired deputy chief who died Friday after a 41-year career that included serving on President George H.W. Bush’s campaign security detail and rescuing residents from a fire.

Ronald L. Fry died peacefully at his home Friday with his wife by his side, the Libertyville Police Department announced in a statement on Monday.

Fry, a longtime Libertyville resident, was 82, according to his obituary.

He was raised in Otway, Ohio, and graduated from Northwest High School before moving to North Chicago to begin a career at Abbott Laboratories, the obituary said.

He was drafted into the U.S. Army in 1965 and served at Fort Knox and in Norfolk, Virginia, where he met his wife.

Fry returned to Lake County after his military service and went back to work at Abbott.

His life changed course when a friend on the Libertyville Police Department took him on a ride-along and he was “instantly hooked,” the obituary said.

Fry went on to dedicate 41 years to law enforcement in Libertyville, serving as a patrolman, equestrian officer, detective, juvenile officer, crime prevention officer, patrol sergeant and patrol lieutenant before retiring as deputy chief.

He was a familiar face at Libertyville High School athletic events and dances and had a special passion for working with young people as a juvenile officer, police officials said.

His obituary described him as “a steady guiding light for young people seeking direction.”

Fry served on the campaign security detail for President George H.W. Bush during his career, the department said.

He entered a smoke-filled, burning building at the Cook Apartments just six months before he retired and rescued and evacuated numerous residents, the obituary said.

Fry received the Life Saving Medal for the rescue and was recognized as an honorary firefighter for his bravery and dedication, the department said.

His obituary said he loved coaching his sons in baseball and softball and led their teams to numerous championships.

Fry enjoyed being an active, devoted grandfather in his later years and treasured afternoons teaching his granddaughter how to fix things in the garage and playing catch with his grandson, his obituary said.

He was happiest when his entire family was gathered under one roof, “preferably with a box of his favorite donuts nearby,” which his wife kept in steady supply even as his mobility declined, the obituary said.

“Beyond his badge, Ron was a devoted husband, father, and grandfather whose legacy of service, integrity, and compassion will not be forgotten,” the police department said.

The department said his impact on the agency and the community he spent a lifetime protecting “will continue to be remembered.”

The department extended its condolences to Fry’s wife, family, friends and all those who worked alongside him and said it was grateful for his many years of service.

“Rest in peace, Deputy Chief Fry. Your service, sacrifice, and legacy will never be forgotten,” police officials said.

Fry is survived by his wife of 60 years, two sons, two daughters-in-law and two grandchildren.

A visitation will be held from 4-7:30 p.m. Wednesday at McMurrough Funeral Chapel in Libertyville.

A prayer service with military honors will follow at 7:30 p.m.
